## The Problem

For a long time I was shipping features fast — too fast. I had GitHub Copilot, ChatGPT, and every AI tool in my workflow. I could go from zero to a working feature in an hour. It felt like a superpower.

Then a teammate asked me to explain a piece of my own code. I couldn't.

## What Over-Reliance Actually Looks Like

It's subtle. You don't notice it until someone puts you on the spot:

- You accept generated code without reading it carefully
- You can't debug without asking AI first
- You don't know _why_ something works, only _that_ it works
- Your mental model of the system is shallow

## How I Fixed It

**1. Read before accepting.** I started treating AI suggestions like a pull request from a junior dev — read every line before merging.

**2. Explain it out loud.** If I couldn't explain a function to myself in plain English, I didn't ship it.

**3. Deliberate practice without AI.** I spent a week writing Python without autocomplete. It was painful and exactly what I needed.

**4. Build real projects from scratch.** Not tutorials, not templates — real projects where _I_ made every architectural decision.

## What I Learned

AI tools are multipliers. If your fundamentals are weak, they multiply shallow understanding. If your fundamentals are strong, they multiply your output.

The engineers who will thrive with AI are the ones who use it as a tool, not a crutch — who can critically evaluate its output because they understand the domain deeply.

Be that engineer.
